## Local development

This is a static site. The homepage and case studies use hand-written CSS. The Jinjalume component gallery uses committed Tailwind CSS, so GitHub Pages does not need a build service.

```bash
npm ci
npm run build
python3 -m http.server 8765
```

Open `http://localhost:8765`. Homepage edits need no build. After changing utility classes in the Jinjalume gallery, run `npm run build` and commit the generated CSS. Hand-written styles live in `styles/site.css`, `jinjalume/site.css`, and `case-studies/styles.css`; the hero garden lives in `garden.js`.

Project screenshots are stored locally in `assets/projects/`; capture sources are documented there. The contact form collects a reply number or username when WhatsApp or Telegram is selected. With JavaScript disabled, it submits through FormSubmit’s standard form endpoint using email as the reply channel.

## Visual design

The homepage is an anime-style Japanese garden. The hero is a live Three.js scene in `garden.js`, cel-shaded with ink outlines: a vermilion torii gate, three mossy stones in raked sand, and red, orange, and gold maple leaves (momiji) that drift down and leave ripples where they land. Moving a pointer across the sand, or tapping it on a phone, rakes new ripples. Behind it is an anime sky with drifting clouds by day, and stars and a moon by night. The sand grooves, moss, and shadows are drawn per pixel on a single plane, the scene pauses when it scrolls out of view or the tab is hidden, and reduced-motion visitors get a still, fully raked garden. Without WebGL or JavaScript, a CSS drawing of rings around a stone takes its place. Three.js 0.170.0 loads as an ES module from jsDelivr. Once the hero scrolls away, a few maple leaves keep drifting behind the page content.

Below the garden, the voyage (`voyage.js`) is a pinned, scroll-driven 3D scene in the style of Apple's product pages. As you scroll through it, a small wooden boat with a battened sail bearing the studio cat sails through a floating torii toward Mount Fuji, and the camera circles it through a sequence of shots. Four captions fade in and out along the way, the numbered chapter buttons jump to each part, and tapping the water sends rings across it. By day the sun sinks toward dusk as the voyage ends; at night a lantern lights the boat. With reduced motion or without JavaScript, the voyage becomes a static set of caption cards over the scene. The garden and the voyage share their tone ramp, ink outlines, torii, and leaf shape through `scene-kit.js`.

Every other section responds to scrolling as well (`script.js` sets `--enter`, `--p`, and `--exit` on each `[data-scroll]` element, and the CSS turns those into motion): project cards tilt up out of the page and lean toward the pointer, kanji seals stamp in while their dashed ring turns and a star orbits them, company tiles swing open like doors, the About text lights up word by word, skill tags and gallery photos fly in, the contact panel tips forward as its moon rises and its waves roll, and the footer seal spins into place. All of it is skipped for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

The palette is indigo ink, torii crimson, gold, steel, sky blue, and jade, with a vermilion hanko seal carrying the studio cat. Headings use Shippori Mincho B1 and body text uses Zen Maru Gothic. The interface borrows from anime and manga: inked outlines with hard offset shadows, a speech-bubble greeting, a vertical nobori banner with the name in katakana, bilingual navigation, and seigaiha wave patterns on the contact panel and footer. Each section is named by a kanji in the left margin, with its reading and meaning: 作 (to make) for works, 守 (to look after) for client work, 縁 (the ties between people) for companies, 人 (a person) for About, 試 (to try) for experiments, and 文 (a letter) for contact. The light/dark toggle follows the system setting until a visitor chooses. Case studies share the palette and type in `case-studies/styles.css`.

The companies and affiliations section contains the original nine organizations, with logos stored locally in `assets/` and `assets/companies/`. The gallery, experience, experiments, and contact form use native expandable sections. Navigation and expandable content remain usable without JavaScript.
